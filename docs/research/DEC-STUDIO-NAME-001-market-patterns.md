## Observations

- One- and two-word structures dominate the coded sample; three-or-more-word forms remain visible, especially where corporate descriptors are retained.
- `Games`, `Studio/Studios`, `Entertainment`, `Interactive` and `Digital` recur, but the absence of a suffix is the largest single coding group.
- Coined and compound-word forms occur in every scale group; this is a frequency observation, not evidence of distinctiveness or suitability.
- Large-company rows more often retain corporate descriptors or group structures; independent rows more often use compact two-word or compound forms. The coding does not prove causation.
- Rebrand events in the event quota show corporate scope changes, ownership/portfolio restructuring, spelling modernization and parent-company identity separation as documented contexts.
- Naming-problem events show recurring conflict contexts: trademark disputes, third-party rights, title confusion, licensing separation, and culturally/organizationally driven renaming.
- Regional pronunciation and script effects cannot be concluded from company-page evidence alone. East Asian brand Romanization and mixed English/local-language source access require a separate Localization review.

## Limitations

Name-form coding is analyst classification and awaits independent audit. Historical cause descriptions are deliberately broad because the source register has not yet received independent content review. No legal inference or future naming instruction follows from these frequencies.
